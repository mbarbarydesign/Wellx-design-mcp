# Copy & tone of voice — Wellx brand language

How Wellx sounds, for anyone who writes, designs, or builds anything a user will see or hear:
UI copy, notifications, onboarding, health content, error states, emails. Wellx operates
across the GCC (UAE, Qatar, Saudi Arabia), the Philippines, and Canada — one voice everywhere,
tone flexes by context. **Every piece of user-facing text an agent writes follows this topic.**
(Wellx Labs B2B insurance surfaces follow `labs-voice` instead; this topic governs Wellx
wellness products.)

**Core belief:** Wellx exists because healthy people build healthier companies. Every employee
is a capable adult who deserves clear, honest, and respectful communication about their own health.

## The four voice traits
1. **Warm** — genuine care, not corporate wellness theatre. Speak like a knowledgeable
   colleague, not a benefits brochure. Acknowledge difficulty without catastrophising.
   Celebrate progress quietly — no confetti for completing a form. Never hollow encouragement
   ("Amazing!", "You're crushing it!").
2. **Clear** — plain language that respects the user's time. One idea per sentence; active
   voice by default. Define health terms on first use if there is any doubt. Most important
   information first. Never hide bad news in small print or gentle language.
3. **Honest** — say what we mean. No over-promised outcomes ("improve your health in 7 days").
   Acknowledge uncertainty in health content where it exists. Errors say what happened in
   plain terms — no blame, no jargon. No dark patterns: fake urgency, misleading labels, guilt.
4. **Empowering** — hand control to the user. Choices and information, never mandates.
   Progress is personal, not competitive. Never frame health as a company KPI the employee must hit.

## Tone by context (with rewrites)
- **Onboarding** — welcoming, short, value without over-selling; assume they are busy.
  "Welcome to Wellx! Your journey starts NOW!" → **"You're in. Let's personalise your
  experience so Wellx works for you."** · "Complete your profile to unlock all the amazing
  features!" → **"A few details help us show you what's most relevant."**
- **Health tracking & nudges** — brief, low-pressure, easy to act on or dismiss. Never shame,
  never guilt. "You haven't logged your mood yet today!" → **"How are you feeling today?
  Takes 10 seconds."** · "Don't break your streak!" → **"You've logged 6 days in a row.
  Keep it going, or pick up tomorrow."**
- **Women's Health** — clinical accuracy with emotional intelligence; never minimise,
  dramatise, or assume. Prefer "mood swings or anxiety" over "depression" outside clinical
  contexts. No exclamation marks on milestone screens. "Your body, your journey" is the
  established banner anchor. "Congratulations on completing your perimenopause check-in!" →
  **"Check-in complete. Your tracker is up to date."** · "Fight back against the symptoms of
  menopause" → **"Understand what's changing, and what you can do about it."**
- **Telemedicine & clinical** — quieter, precise; clinical accuracy first; no marketing
  language at all. "Chat with an amazing doctor right now!" → **"Connect with a licensed
  doctor, available now."**
- **Error states** — honest, what happened + what to do; never blame the user for technical
  failures. "Oops! Something went wrong." → **"We couldn't load your data. Check your
  connection and try again, or contact support if this keeps happening."** · "Invalid input."
  → **"A few fields need updating, highlighted below."**
- **Success states** — acknowledge simply; match celebration to significance. "AMAZING! You
  did it! 🎉🎉🎉" → **"Done. Your details have been saved."** · "You're a wellness superstar!"
  → **"Programme complete. Great work over the past four weeks."**

## Terminology (use → not)
- **wellness** → wellbeing, health & wellness (single ownable term)
- **doctor / physician** → medic, healthcare provider
- **mood tracking** → mood diary, emotional check-in
- **your journey** → your programme, your plan
- **Women's Health** → women's wellness, female health
- **consultation** → appointment, visit, session
- **streak** → consecutive days, daily record
- **activation** → sign-up, registration (internal/metric language only)
- **check in** → log / track (task language) · **available** → unlock (gamification) ·
  **personalise** → tailor / customise (marketing voice) · **contact support** → reach out
  (informal in GCC) · **low mood** → "mental health struggles" (too vague) ·
  **we couldn't load this** → oops / uh-oh (trivialises errors) · **complete** → done!/amazing!

## Multi-market
- **GCC** — formal register in B2B, warmer in app UI. No colloquial slang ("crushing it",
  "hustle", "vibe"). Tracking framed as personal, never communal. Ramadan-aware streak logic
  and notification cadence. Prefer "contact" over "reach out".
- **Philippines** — conversational English; contractions fine; slightly higher energy than
  GCC. No US-centric health-insurance framing. Wellness as self-care, not corporate performance.
- **Canada** — Canadian spelling (behaviour, personalise, centre). Unambiguous copy without
  idiomatic dependency (bilingual awareness). Direct, low-context. Healthcare is a right, not
  a perk — never frame corporate wellness as a luxury.

## Punctuation & formatting
- **Exclamation marks**: none on success screens, completion states, or health milestones.
  Reserve for genuine urgency or delight — once, deliberately.
- **Case**: UI labels, buttons, headings, navigation = sentence case. Product feature names =
  Title Case (Women's Health, Mood Tracking, Wellx Kids).
- **Contractions**: use them ("You're", "We couldn't") — except in formal clinical summaries
  and legal/compliance copy.
- **Numbers**: spell out one–nine, numerals for 10+. Percentages always "%". Time 10:00 AM /
  3:30 PM, no seconds unless relevant.
- **Em dashes**: avoid in UI copy, notifications, and most product writing — replace with a
  comma or split the sentence. Reserve for long-form editorial where a strong pause is intentional.
- **Emoji**: contextual, never decorative; one can add warmth, five is noise. Never in
  clinical or error contexts; never as punctuation or sarcasm.

## What Wellx is not
A knowledgeable colleague, not a hype machine ("Transform your life!") · calmly encouraging,
not a guilt machine ("You haven't tracked in 3 days") · clinically precise, not vague ·
honest about uncertainty, never over-promising ("Guaranteed results") · respectful of
autonomy, never paternalistic ("You should really…") · low-pressure day-to-day, urgent only
when there is urgency · professionally warm, never casually unprofessional in B2B.

## Quick reference — before shipping copy, ask
Is this the shortest it can be while still clear? · Any hollow praise or false urgency? ·
Does it treat the user as a capable adult? · Any promise we can't keep? · Is the tone right
for this context (clinical, casual, celebratory)? · Is every exclamation mark earned? ·
Does it work for GCC, the Philippines, AND Canada? · Any word that could feel dismissive?
